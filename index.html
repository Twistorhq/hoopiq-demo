The workflow

Teaching Timeouts

A teaching timeout is a normal timeout, used better. The coach opens the possession that just ended, sees it overlaid against the play design, and sends the team back out with one clear correction per player.

STEP 1

Capture

The game records from the camera already in the gym, mounted high with the full floor in frame. No new hardware crew, no gimbal, no leased camera.

STEP 2

Track

Every player is tracked through the possession: where they went, when they moved, who was open, who was late.

STEP 3

Compare

The play design and basketball principles set the expected positions. The overlay draws actual against expected, possession by possession.

STEP 4

Teach

At the timeout, the coach shows the clip and the overlay on a tablet. The correction lands before the next possession, not at tomorrow’s film session.

Playcall overlay

Call it, see it, run it.

Before the team breaks the huddle, the coach selects the next play and the routes animate on the court view: screens, cuts, and spacing drawn exactly where each player needs to be. Youth and middle school players learn by seeing it on the floor they are standing on.

The same overlay powers the correction after the play: design on one layer, reality on the other, one screenshot that explains both.

Football formations are known before the snap, so expected assignments are exact: every player has a job on the call, and the overlay scores what actually happened against it.

Second core sport

Football, from the same engine.

Basketball and football share one core: capture, tracking, expected vs actual, teaching. Football adds a formation layer. The system reads the pre-snap formation, attaches the expected assignment for each player from the play call, then reports deviation and tendency after the snap: who bit on the fake, who held contain, which way the run was supposed to go and where it actually went.

Formations give the expected side of the overlay deterministic inputs, which makes football the natural second core while basketball principles cover the continuous game.

The rollout

90 days: November 6, 2026 to February 4, 2027

HoopIQ is in a 90-day build and rollout program aimed at high school and college programs, built with the schools, districts, and leagues that run these sports. Partnership data agreements put real games into the dataset the right way, with school consent, before the pro leagues move down into this level.

Phase 1: Foundation

Infrastructure first

Repository, data layer, and hosting foundations, plus camera-agnostic ingest for the cameras already mounted in gyms and stadiums.

Phase 2: Basketball pilot

Teaching Timeouts on real games

Partner programs record games; possession breakdowns, expected vs actual overlays, and timeout corrections run with real coaching staffs.

Phase 3: Football beta

Formation layer on recorded games

Formation recognition, expected assignments, deviation and tendency reports, validated on recorded football games.

Day 90: February 4, 2027

Rollout milestone

Basketball pilot programs live, football beta demonstrated end to end, and the dataset partnerships in place to grow both.
